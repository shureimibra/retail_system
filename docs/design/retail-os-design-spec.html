Design spec · Draft 3

Every customer who walks in leaves a trace

Retail OS starts with one job, for any kind of shop: when it can't sell someone what they came for, it records why, gets the item, tells the customer on WhatsApp with a photo, talks them through to a deposit or payment, and counts the sale only when the money is in. This spec covers that workflow, the look, the screens and the build order.

Date 22 Oct 2026 Draft 3 restores the draft 1 look; keeps the draft 2 workflow Status Waiting on the open decisions at the end
01 · The business problem

One customer, eight steps, any kind of shop

You described this with a shoe shop, and the same steps hold for a phone shop, a pharmacy or a hardware store. Only the details of "exactly what they wanted" change, so the system stores those details generically, as options on a product (size, colour, storage, strength, pack size and so on).

Take a shoe shop. A customer loves a shoe but we don't have size 42 in black. Today they walk out and the sale is gone. With Retail OS, they walk out and we keep the sale alive.
1 · Walk-inEvery visit gets an outcome

They either buy, or staff log why not: size, colour, out of stock, price, budget, or another reason.

2 · CapturePhone, WhatsApp, email

Staff record the exact item, the reason, how soon they need it ("give me 3–4 days") and consent to be contacted.

3 · MorningThe to-order list

The owner sees what to buy and who is waiting for each item, ranked by money waiting and promise dates.

4 · OrderMark as ordered

Optional: set an arrival date, and customers can get an "arriving Thursday" update.

5 · RestockStock in, messages out

Receiving stock holds units for waiting customers and sends each a WhatsApp with a photo of the exact item. SMS and email are fallbacks.

6 · Follow upUp to four tries

No reply? Follow-ups go out on a schedule and stop the moment they answer or opt out.

7 · TalkReplies in one inbox

Staff chat with the customer: change the colour, agree a pickup day, take a deposit, hold the item.

8 · PaidRecovered

Only a full payment counts as recovered revenue. Promises and deposits are tracked separately.

02 · Lifecycle

What "notified", "reserved" and "recovered" mean

Each stage has one rule for entering it. Notified only means the message arrived. The customer's reply, the deposit and the payment are separate stages.

Open

Logged. Waiting for stock, or a price change on price and budget reasons.

→
On order

Optional. Owner marked it ordered, with an expected date.

→
Back in stock

Stock received. A unit is held and the message is queued.

→
Notified

Message delivered. Follow-ups scheduled until they reply.

→
In conversation

Customer replied. They may change size, colour or item.

→
Reserved

Deposit paid or hold agreed, with a pickup-by date. Pipeline, not revenue.

→
Recovered

Paid in full. The only stage counted as recovered revenue.

Declined

Customer said no ("bought elsewhere"). Closed with a reason, not revenue. Held stock is released.

Expired

No reply after the last follow-up, a reservation not collected in time, or 30 days with no stock. Held stock is released.

Send failed

A flag on the message, not a stage (wrong number, not on WhatsApp). Stays until someone fixes it or picks another channel.

Follow-up schedule (default, editable per shop)

  • Day 0: restock message with the item's photo. Day 2: reminder. Day 4: "still holding it". Day 7: last call.
  • Any reply pauses the schedule and moves the record to In conversation.
  • STOP or a decline ends it at once. No reply after day 7 makes it Expired.

When the customer changes their mind

  • "Do you have it in brown?" Staff use Change item; the original request stays in the history.
  • If the new item is in stock it can go straight to Reserved; if not, the record goes back to Open for the new item.
  • Reports count it once, as a recovery of the original visit.

Price too high and over budget

  • These don't move when stock arrives. They wait for the price of that product or option to drop (a markdown, sale or promotion).
  • When the price drops, the customer gets a message with the old and new price and the item's photo, then the normal flow continues from Notified.
  • Until then they sit in their own "Waiting for a lower price" view, so the owner can see how many sales a price cut would win back.

04 · Principles

Rules every screen is checked against

Work first, decoration never

No glow, blur, gradients or background photos inside the app. Colour carries meaning only: the brand action and status.

Every number is real

Each figure links to the records behind it, and "recovered" means paid.

The next action is obvious

Home is a work queue, not a wall of charts. Each record shows its stage and what moves it on.

Phone first, any screen

One web app for local shop owners on phones, tablets, laptops and shop computers. Designed at 360px first, then expanded. Same tokens, same components.

Built to plug in

Every screen action is also an API action, so the widget and POS integrations reuse the same services.

Local by default

Currency, timezone and phone format come from the shop's settings. KSh and +254 are first-class.

09 · Every screen size

One web app for the phone, the tablet and the shop computer

The people using Retail OS are local shop owners and their staff. Most will open it on an Android phone; some on a tablet at the till or a laptop or desktop in the shop. So it's a web app only, with no app store. Every screen is designed phone-first and then expands to larger screens.

ScreenWidthNavigationHow pages adapt
Phone360–599pxBottom tab bar: Home, Waiting, Chats, Stock, More. Settings sits under MoreOne column. Tables become cards. Floor mode shows one step per screen. Conversations go list → chat → deal sheet
Tablet600–1023pxSlim sidebar with icons and labelsTwo columns. Floor mode shows all three steps side by side in landscape, two in portrait
Laptop and desktop1024px and upFull sidebarFull tables, three-pane inbox, the layouts in section 8

Built for local phones and data

  • Works from 360px wide, the size of common budget Android phones, with no sideways scrolling.
  • Tap targets at least 44px, and 50px+ in floor mode.
  • Pages stay light (under about 200 KB before product photos), so they load on slow mobile data. Product photos are resized thumbnails.
  • Inputs open the right keyboard: number pad for phone and price, email keyboard for email.

Feels like an app without an app store

  • "Add to home screen" gives the shop's icon on the phone and opens full-screen, like an app.
  • Stays signed in on the shop's own devices.
  • Later option: save a missed sale without signal and send it when the connection returns.
  • Every phase is checked at 360, 768 and 1280px before sign-off.
10 · Messaging, free first

Build and test the whole loop for free

WhatsApp is the main channel because it can carry a photo of the exact item. Every channel sits behind one gateway, so moving from free test setups to paid production accounts is a settings change, not a rewrite.

ChannelLocal development ($0)Real-phone testing (free)Production (paid, later)
WhatsApp with photoBuilt-in customer simulatorMeta WhatsApp Cloud API test number (free, a short list of verified phones). Fallback: Twilio sandbox (join code, expires after 3 days)Meta Cloud API on the shop's own number, approved templates
SMSBuilt-in customer simulatorAfrica's Talking sandbox (free; messages appear in their web simulator)Africa's Talking live for Kenya, Twilio SMS elsewhere
EmailMailpit (free local inbox)Brevo free plan (about 300 a day) or Resend (100 a day)Same provider, paid tier
DepositsRecorded by handM-Pesa Daraja sandbox (free)Daraja live: payments confirmed automatically

The customer simulator

A developer-only page where you play the customer: see the WhatsApp they'd get, photo included, and reply "yes", "do you have brown?" or "STOP". Replies go through the same path as a real webhook, so the whole workflow is testable with no account, phone or cost.

Rules the gateway enforces

  • One send path; nothing else talks to a provider.
  • Consent, valid E.164 number and duplicate checks before every send.
  • First WhatsApp uses an approved template; replies within 24 hours can be free text.
  • If WhatsApp fails, offer SMS, then email. STOP works on every channel.

Free-tier limits change often; each will be confirmed on the provider's pricing page during Phase 1.

11 · Design skills

One design authority, no contradictions

The impeccable suite leads, reading PRODUCT.md and DESIGN.md written from this spec. When rules collide: your words, then this spec, then impeccable, then anything else.

Use

  • impeccable, shape: design brief before each screen
  • critique, audit: review after building
  • polish, typeset, layout, clarify, adapt, harden: finishing passes
  • animate: in-app motion. delight, overdrive: only for the landing page and marketing site in the final phase
  • emil-design-eng (principles only), dataviz for charts
  • code-review, simplify, security-review, run for engineering

Don't use here

  • cosmic-glass-dashboard: the neon glass look we're removing
  • design-taste-frontend, gpt-taste, high-end-visual-design, stitch-design-taste, redesign-existing-projects: own font bans, React/GSAP, "never repeat a layout"
  • minimalist-ui, industrial-brutalist-ui, ui-ux-pro-max: different styles or their own design system
  • colorize, bolder: palette is decided
  • image-to-code, imagegen-*, brandkit, website: revisit in Phase 4
12 · Plan

Build order

Each phase: short design note, build on a branch, tests, your sign-off, merge.

Phase 0Clean repo

Stop tracking venv/, __pycache__, .env and the database. Rotate the Twilio token and secret key. Pin requirements, add .env.example, a true README, CI, and the project CLAUDE.md, PRODUCT.md and DESIGN.md.

Done when a fresh clone installs and passes tests.

Phase 1Workflow engine

New lifecycle and data migration. Stock receiving with holds and partial restock. Messaging gateway with the simulator, Mailpit, the WhatsApp Cloud test number and the Africa's Talking sandbox. Follow-ups, inbound replies, hand-recorded deposits, roles. Duplicate services removed.

Done when the eight steps in section 1 run end to end in the simulator, with tests for every stage.

Phase 2Look and shell

Tokens, Plex fonts, components, sidebar, a plain sign-in page, and Settings: account, shop details, look, home layout per role, reasons, follow-ups and holds, messaging channels, team and roles.

Done when the shell works at 360, 768 and 1280px in both themes, and the old neon CSS is deleted.

Phase 3Screens

Home with widgets, Waiting, To order, Conversations, Products with photos, Customers, Reports, Floor mode.

Phase 4Paid channels

Production WhatsApp, live SMS, M-Pesa confirmation.

Phase 5–6Plug-in, then AI

API keys, inventory webhooks, embeddable capture widget. Then MCP server, forecasting, reply-drafting assistant.

LastFront door

Landing page, marketing site and a fully designed sign-in, with their own design round. This starts only after the dashboard and the whole pipeline are finished.

Research with REA (github.com/morluto/rea) runs before Phase 2: Reatech360 for features only, plus Loyverse, Shopify POS and Polaris docs, Shopify Inbox, Odoo Community, Square, Toast and Zoho Inventory public pages. Patterns only, never their branding or code.
